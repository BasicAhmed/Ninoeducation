Footer reorganized into a 2-column link grid, and a real psychology-informed fix to the apply wizard's step order

1. Footer links were stacked one-per-line, 7 deep, making the footer
   noticeably tall. Changed the links list to a 2-column grid and
   moved the dashboard sign-in link into it (it's navigation, not a
   contact method) — same content, roughly half the vertical height.

2. The apply wizard asked about budget and how the student plans to
   fund their training BEFORE asking what license or school they
   actually want. Psychologically, that's backwards for a decision
   this significant: leading with a financial-screening question
   before someone has even stated their goal reads as gatekeeping,
   not service — and it's exactly the kind of moment likely to cause
   drop-off (which matters concretely now that abandoned applications
   are being tracked and followed up on). Reordered the wizard so the
   'what's your goal / which license / which school' step comes
   directly after contact info, with the money step moving after it —
   so by the time someone's asked about budget, they've already
   stated what they're funding, and the existing money-step copy
   ('we want to recommend schools that fit your actual budget') now
   reads as being in service of the goal they just named, not a
   screening gate before it.

This required renumbering three things in lockstep — the STEPS array,
the per-step validation function's index checks, and the JSX panels
themselves (each panel's active/ref index literally matches its
position in the array) — a real risk of a silent step/validation
mismatch if any one of the three drifted from the others.

Verified with a full, corrected end-to-end walkthrough of the
reordered wizard (not just the type-checker): confirmed the step
immediately after contact info is now the goal/license/school step,
confirmed money correctly follows it, confirmed readiness follows
that, and confirmed the final review screen shows all the same data
correctly captured through the new order. Caught and fixed two of my
own test-script mistakes during this — first selecting the empty
placeholder option in a school dropdown, then using stale pre-
formal-tone button text — rather than mistaking either for a real
bug, by checking what was actually rendered before concluding
anything was broken.
